## 1. ⛔ Found before C could be measured: **X1's GREEN was an artifact. 0016 would have failed at X5.**

Creating the C helper exposed it — the brand-new function came out with an explicit **`anon=X`** grant despite following the E-2 pattern.

**Mechanism:** `pg_default_acl` for functions in schema `public` grants EXECUTE to `anon` **explicitly** (`objtype=f → {postgres=X, anon=X, authenticated=X, service_role=X}`). PUBLIC and the explicit anon grant are **two independent channels**. E-2 diagnosed one of them.

**Why X1 passed anyway:** apply *history*. v1 of `0016` ran `revoke … from anon`; v2's `create or replace` **preserves the existing ACL** and additionally revoked PUBLIC — so the pair of applies left it clean. Dropping the helpers and re-applying `0016` fresh brought `anon=X` straight back on all three, with `has_function_privilege('anon', …) = true`. **That is exactly what X5's from-scratch run would have hit.**

**Fixed and verified:** every helper now carries **both** revokes. Fresh-create verification of all four: ACL `{postgres=X, authenticated=X, service_role=X}`, `anon=false`, impersonated anon denied `42501`.
Evidence: `evidence/X2_FINDING_fresh_create_anon_grant.log` · `evidence/X1_AC8_four_helpers_FINAL_2026-09-01.log`

**Applied rather than held** because the ratified AC8 requirement ("anon lacks EXECUTE") was otherwise unmeetable from scratch; it adds no requirement and weakens none. **Ratification of the wording requested** — E-2's literal instruction was `from public` *in place of* `from anon`; the evidence says both. Recorded as **ERRATUM E-4**.

**Method lesson (F-12, drafted at close):** privilege assertions must be made after a **from-scratch** apply — `create or replace` preserves ACLs and can mask a grant defect.
